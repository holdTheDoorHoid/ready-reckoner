## Use this when

Banks close or limit withdrawals, or a bank you use fails. No one has lost a penny of insured deposits since the FDIC began in 1933.[^fdic_when_bank_fails]

## Do first

1. **Use the cash you keep at home** for food, fuel and supplies.[^ready_gov_financial]
2. **Watch your mail** for a letter from the FDIC or the bank that takes over your account.[^fdic_when_bank_fails]
3. **Keep paying your loans** as agreed.[^fdic_borrowers_guide]
4. **Call the FDIC at 1-877-275-3342** with questions about your deposits.[^fdic_deposit_insurance_faq]
5. **Find your account list** and bank phone numbers.[^fema_effak] {ref:documents}

## Then

1. **Expect your insured money within a few days,** usually the next business day, in a new account at another bank or by check.[^fdic_deposit_insurance_faq]
2. **Follow the payment instructions** in the FDIC's letter for any loan from the failed bank.[^fdic_borrowers_guide]
3. **Pay with cash** in small bills while card readers or cash machines are down.[^ready_gov_financial]

## Leave or stay

- **Do this if** you have more than $250,000 at one bank: call the FDIC to check your coverage.[^fdic_deposit_insurance_faq]
- **Do this if** you cannot pay a loan from the failed bank: ask the FDIC about a loan workout.[^fdic_borrowers_guide]

## Where and who

## Do not

- Do not give account numbers, PINs or passwords to anyone who calls, texts or emails you.[^ready_gov_cybersecurity]
- Do not take a payday loan. A typical two-week loan costs as much as almost 400 percent a year.[^cfpb_payday_loans]

## When it is over

- Keep some cash at home in small bills.[^ready_gov_financial]
- Keep your deposits within the insured amount, at least $250,000 at each FDIC-insured bank.[^fdic_deposit_insurance]
