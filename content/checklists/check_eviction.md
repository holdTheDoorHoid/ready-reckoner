## Use this when

Your landlord gives you a notice to pay or leave, or you get court papers for an eviction.

## Do first

1. **Call legal aid or your local bar association.** You may qualify for free legal help.[^cfpb_facing_eviction][^lsc_get_legal_help]
2. **Call the court clerk.** Ask the deadline to file an answer, the court date, and whether there is mediation.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
3. **File a written answer** by the deadline, with a lawyer or on your own.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
4. **Apply for rent help.** Call 211 or your local government.[^cfpb_rent_help]
5. **Go to court on your court date.**[^cfpb_facing_eviction]

## Then

1. **Talk with your landlord** about a plan to catch up on rent.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
2. **Tell the court** in your answer if you applied for rent help.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
3. **Call a HUD-approved housing counselor** at 1-800-569-4287.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
4. **Keep your important papers together** in case you have to move.[^fema_effak] {ref:documents}

## Leave or stay

- **Do this if** the court rules against you: ask the judge or clerk to put the order on hold while your rent help is processed.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
- **Do this if** you have to move: apply for help with moving costs, a deposit and application fees.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]
- **Do this if** you are in the military: call your Legal Assistance Office. You can ask the court to pause the case for up to 90 days.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]

## Do not

- Do not ignore court papers. Too many renters give up before they have a chance to go to court.[^cfpb_facing_eviction]

## When it is over

- Call or text 988 if the stress is too much.[^samhsa_988]
