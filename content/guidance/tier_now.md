Free actions come first in every plan, because they cost nothing and cover a lot. In FEMA's 2024 survey, cost was the most common reason people gave for not preparing, and "I don't know what else to do" came second.[^fema_nhs_2024] This tier answers both.

**What is in it.** Turn on emergency alerts on every phone.[^ready_gov_alerts] Write each person a contact card, and pick an out-of-state contact.[^ready_gov_low_cost][^ready_gov_earthquakes] Fill clean bottles with tap water.[^cdc_water_storage] Test smoke alarms every month.[^usfa_smoke_alarms] Refill medicines early, and keep a written list of them.[^ready_gov_disability] Photograph your belongings and copy key documents.[^fema_effak] Swap phone numbers with two neighbours. About 7 in 10 people expect help from friends and family, but fewer than 2 in 10 had heard how to help their neighbours.[^fema_nhs_2024] Washington's guide also starts with a free step: talk with your family and make a plan.[^washington_prepare_in_a_year]

**When it is enough.** This tier is done when each free action applies to your household and is checked off. Free actions stay in the plan as you move on. Drills and checks come back in the maintenance calendar.
