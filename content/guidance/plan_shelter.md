Pick a spot for each danger below, at home and at work, and practice going there.[^ready_gov_plan]

**Strong wind.** In a high wind or severe thunderstorm warning, go inside a sturdy building, to an inside room{if:not_home:apartment_low_rise|apartment_high_rise} or basement{/if}.[^nws_high_wind_during] Stay away from windows while damaging wind is near.[^nws_severe_weather_during] {if:home:mobile_home}From a mobile home, go to a sturdy building before the wind picks up.[^nws_high_wind_during]{/if}

{if:tornado}**Tornado.** A safe room or storm shelter is best; next is a small inside room with no windows on the lowest floor.[^ready_gov_tornadoes] Even a tied-down mobile home is not safe.[^noaa_spc_tornado_safety]{/if}

{if:hurricane}**Hurricane.** If officials tell your evacuation zone to leave, go right away. If you stay, use an inside room away from windows.[^ready_gov_hurricanes]{/if} {if:home:apartment_high_rise}In a tall building, take shelter on or below the 10th floor when hurricane winds come, and do not count on the elevators.[^fema_hurricane_safety_tips_2012][^nws_hurricane_safety_brochure]{/if}

{if:earthquake}**Earthquake.** Drop where you are, cover your head and neck, and hold on. Crawl under a sturdy table, or next to an inside wall away from windows. Do not run outside.[^ready_gov_earthquakes]{/if}

{if:hazmat_release}**Chemical release.** If told to stay inside, go to an above-ground room with few openings. Close the windows, doors and vents, turn off fans and air conditioning, and seal the room with plastic sheeting and duct tape.[^ready_gov_chemical]{/if}

{if:wildfire_smoke}**Smoke.** Make one room a clean room: close its windows and doors, and run an air cleaner that does not make ozone.[^epa_clean_room]{/if}

**Radiation.** If officials warn of fallout, get inside, stay inside, stay tuned. Go to a basement or the middle of a brick or concrete building.[^ready_gov_nuclear]
