Your plan puts your county in one of six groups for nuclear war, from next to a missile field to far from all such places. Here is why we say it, what it means and what it does not.

**Why we say it.** The Air Force expects its missile fields to be targets in a nuclear war. Princeton researchers mapped where fallout from them could land, and said people nearby need to know.[^missilesonourland_2023] Each place we name is on a public list, such as the Defense Department's military sites, the national nuclear labs and plants, or the Census Bureau's largest metro areas.[^dod_mirta_points][^nnsa_locations][^census_cbsa_pop_2024] We show the source for every site.[^rr_design_decision_log_2026]

**What it means.** Your group tells you how badly a war could reach you. Fallout travels downwind and is most dangerous in the first hours. Getting inside a sturdy building while it weakens can save many lives.[^fema_nuclear_72h_2023] Who gets fallout on a given day depends on the winds.[^missilesonourland_2023] So in groups 1 to 5, your plan adds one free step: pick your shelter spot at home and at work.[^ready_gov_nuclear]

**What it does not mean.** It does not mean an attack is likely. In a 2024 survey, experts put the chance of a nuclear catastrophe anywhere in the world before 2045 at about 5 in 100.[^fri_nuclear_risk_2024] It does not ask you to move or to buy anything. Your three days of basics cover the first days in every group.[^ready_gov_nuclear][^fema_nuclear_72h_2023]
