An electric car can carry you out of harm's way if it has enough charge and a route that fits its range. Most electric cars can travel more than 100 miles on a charge, and some can go more than 200 or 400 miles, depending on the model.[^doe_fueleconomy_ev]

**What helps.** If your evacuation plan includes leaving in your electric car, keep it fully charged during hurricane season, so it is ready when an order comes.[^hcfl_ev_safety] Know how far your car can go, and plan routes and charging stops that fit.[^hcfl_ev_safety] A federal station locator shows chargers along a route.[^doe_afdc_stations] A full recharge can take 3 to 12 hours, and even a fast charge to 80% takes about 30 minutes, so leave early.[^doe_fueleconomy_ev]

**If you leave the car behind.** One Florida county advises keeping the battery at about 30% charge, parking at least 50 feet from buildings and other cars, and leaving it unplugged. Floodwater, especially salt water, can start a battery fire.[^hcfl_ev_safety]

**What to avoid.** Do not drive or charge an electric car that has been in floodwater. A battery fire can start right away, or weeks or months later.[^hcfl_ev_safety]
