{frequency} Storms, power cuts and computer failures can knock out phones, internet and card readers. When networks are busy, a text message may get through when a call does not, and it saves battery.[^ready_gov_earthquakes]

**What helps.** Turn on emergency alerts on every phone. They are free, and you do not need to sign up.[^ready_gov_alerts] Keep a battery or hand-crank radio and a NOAA Weather Radio with a tone alert.[^ready_gov_kit] NOAA Weather Radio has more than 1,000 transmitters covering all 50 states.[^nws_weather_radio] Write important phone numbers on paper, not only in your phone.[^ready_gov_low_cost] Pick an out-of-state contact everyone can text, and a place to meet if you are separated.[^ready_gov_earthquakes] Keep some cash in small bills, because ATMs and credit cards may not work.[^ready_gov_financial] License-free two-way radios (FRS) let family members talk over short distances without a phone network.[^fcc_frs]

**If your phone is lost or broken.** One phone can be a single point of failure. Know two or three numbers by heart, keep a charger in your bag, and know how to sign in to your accounts without your phone, using backup codes kept on paper or with someone you trust.[^ollam_2022_lawyer_passport_locksmith_gun]

**What to avoid.** Do not rely on one source of news. Do not assume you can text 911 everywhere. It is not available in every area, so call 911 if you can.[^fcc_text_911]
