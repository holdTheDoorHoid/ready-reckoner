{frequency} There were about 345,000 home fires in the United States in 2023.[^usfa_residential_fires] Cooking is by far the leading cause of home fires and home fire injuries.[^usfa_cooking_fires]

**What helps.** Put smoke alarms inside and outside sleeping areas and on every level of your home. Test them every month.[^usfa_smoke_alarms] Some fire departments and the Red Cross install them for free.[^usfa_smoke_alarms][^redcross_sound_the_alarm] If you rent, ask your landlord to put them in.[^usfa_smoke_alarm_renters] Make an escape plan with two ways out of each room, and practice it twice a year.[^ready_gov_home_fires] Cooking left alone is the top cause of kitchen fires that spread beyond the pan, so stay with your cooking. Keep a pan lid or baking sheet nearby to cover a pan that catches fire.[^usfa_cooking_fires] Keep anything that can burn at least 3 feet from heaters, fireplaces and candles.[^usfa_heating_fires] A multipurpose extinguisher labeled "A-B-C" works on most home fires.[^usfa_extinguishers]

**What to avoid.** If a person or pet is still inside, get out, call 911, and tell firefighters where they are.[^ready_gov_home_fires] Never heat your home with an oven.[^usfa_heating_fires] Turn off space heaters when you leave the room or go to bed.[^usfa_heating_fires] Replace 9-volt alarm batteries at least once a year.[^usfa_smoke_alarms] If you smell gas or hear hissing, get everyone out without touching switches or phones, then call 911 and the gas company from a safe distance.[^pa_puc_gas_emergencies][^ready_gov_safety_skills] Only a professional should turn the gas back on.[^ready_gov_safety_skills]
