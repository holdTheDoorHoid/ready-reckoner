Disasters are harder when you rely on power, medicine, equipment or other people. In FEMA's 2024 survey, 16 of every 100 people said needing help because of health or a disability kept them from preparing.[^fema_nhs_2024] A plan built around your needs closes that gap.

**What helps.** Build a support network of people who can help, and keep their contact list in a watertight container.[^ready_gov_disability] Make sure at least one of them has a key to your home, knows where your supplies are, and knows how to use your equipment.[^ready_gov_older_adults] Talk to your doctor or pharmacist about an emergency supply of medicines.[^ready_gov_disability] If equipment needs electricity, plan backup power, and ask your power company to put you on its priority restoration list.[^ready_gov_disability] Buy an extra battery for a power wheelchair, or find a local group that can help you get one. If you are on dialysis, know more than one facility that can treat you. If you have a communication disability, carry printed cards that tell responders how to communicate with you.[^ready_gov_disability] Some local emergency offices keep a register for people who may need help; ask yours.[^ready_gov_disability]

**What to avoid.** Do not plan to evacuate by car if you do not have one. Arrange your ride ahead of time.[^ready_gov_older_adults]
