Some companies sell "emergency antibiotic kits" for disaster preparedness. Ready Reckoner never sets an antibiotic quantity. Here is why, and what to do instead.

**What the kits are.** By federal law, antibiotics for people need a prescription.[^usc_21_353] The kits are sold through an online questionnaire that a prescriber signs off, and they typically cost $300 to $400. A 2026 medical review found that this model prescribes for an illness no one has diagnosed. That invites the wrong drug, the wrong dose and the wrong length of treatment. It can also cause side effects and resistant infections.[^mo_med_2026_antibiotic_kits]

**When a standby prescription can make sense.** CDC notes that travelers often carry some prescribed medicines for specific risks where they are going.[^cdc_yellow_book_travel_kits] The same review suggests telehealth with a clinician who knows you, and keeping prescriptions made in advance for specific high-risk cases.[^mo_med_2026_antibiotic_kits] If a specific situation worries you, ask your own clinician.

**What to avoid.** Do not save antibiotics for later, share them, or take someone else's.[^cdc_antibiotic_use] Do not use expired antibiotics. They can fail to treat an infection.[^fda_expired_medicines] Never use aquarium, bird or other animal antibiotics. The FDA calls these products unapproved and unsafe.[^fda_fish_antibiotics_warning_2023]
