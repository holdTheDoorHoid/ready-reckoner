If local officials ask you to evacuate, your pets should evacuate too.[^ready_gov_pets] Most public shelters take only service animals, so plan where your pets would stay.[^ready_gov_evacuation]

**What helps.** Find pet-friendly places to stay ahead of time, such as a friend's home or a pet-friendly motel.[^aspca_disaster_prep][^ready_gov_evacuation] Pack a pet go-kit: food in an airtight, waterproof container, a water bowl and several days of water, extra medicine, and litter or bags.[^ready_gov_pets] The ASPCA suggests 7 to 10 days of food, at least 7 days of water, a two-week supply of medicine, a carrier for each pet, records and recent photos.[^aspca_disaster_prep] A dog drinks about 1 ounce of water per pound of body weight each day.[^petmd_dog_water] The plan sizes cat water from a standard formula for animals' daily fluid needs.[^merck_vet_maintenance_fluids] Put a rescue-alert sticker on your home so rescuers know pets are inside.[^aspca_disaster_prep] The ASPCA also recommends microchipping your pets.[^aspca_disaster_prep]

**What to avoid.** Do not leave pets behind, even for what seems like a short evacuation. Plan as if you might be away for weeks.[^aspca_disaster_prep] Never leave a pet in a closed car on a warm day.[^ready_gov_heat]
