{frequency} Sometimes you cannot get to a store, or the shelves are empty. Winter storms, runs on stores before a storm, curfews and disease outbreaks can all do this. In spring 2020, statewide stay-at-home orders lasted about six weeks in the middle state.[^cdc_mmwr_stay_at_home_2020][^rr_research_risk_model]

**What helps.** Keep extra food that needs no refrigeration, cooking or water, and choose foods your family will eat.[^ready_gov_food] Use the oldest cans first and replace them as you go.[^fsis_shelf_stable] Buy a little at a time. Ready.gov asks people to buy supplies slowly, so everyone can get what they need.[^ready_gov_pandemic] Keep a manual can opener.[^ready_gov_food] The plan counts food in calories, not servings. Many "30-day" meal kits hold only about 1,300 to 1,700 calories a day.[^rr_research_supply_standards] That is less than most adults need.[^usda_dga_2020_2025] Store food somewhere cool and dry. Below 85°F is best.[^fsis_shelf_stable]

**What to avoid.** Do not buy everything at once. It strains your budget and the store. Do not buy or eat from cans that bulge, leak, are rusted or are deeply dented.[^fsis_shelf_stable] Do not trust a kit's "days" label. Check the calories on the package instead.
