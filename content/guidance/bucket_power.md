{frequency} Most outages last a few hours. In 2024 the average home lost power for about 11 hours in total, mostly because of hurricanes.[^eia_outage_hours_2024] After recent hurricanes, restoring power to 95 of every 100 customers who lost it took about 3 days to nearly 3 weeks.[^ornl_repowrd_2022]

**What helps.** Have a flashlight for each person.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Keep a battery or hand-crank radio, and a backup battery for each phone.[^ready_gov_kit] Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours, and a full freezer for about two days.[^ready_gov_power_outages] If someone relies on a powered medical device or cold medicine, make a plan with their medical provider now.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Join your power company's medical program for early warnings of outages, but plan as if your power will not come back first.[^psegli_critical_care] Many cities and counties keep a voluntary registry of people who may need help; ask yours.[^ready_gov_disability] A battery power station burns no fuel, so it makes no carbon monoxide.[^cdc_co_basics]

**What to avoid.** Never run a generator inside your home or garage, even with the doors and windows open. Keep it outside, more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents.[^cdc_co_basics] Put a carbon monoxide alarm on every level of your home.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion can mean carbon monoxide poisoning. Get everyone outside to fresh air, then call 911.[^cpsc_co_information_center] Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven. Unplug electronics, because the power can come back with a surge that damages them.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Treat every downed power line as live: stay at least 30 feet from it and anything it touches, and call 911.[^pa_puc_power_line_safety] If one falls on your car, stay inside unless the car is on fire.[^osha_downed_wires]
