{frequency} Most outages last a few hours. In 2024 the average home lost power for about 11 hours in total, mostly because of hurricanes.[^eia_outage_hours_2024] After recent hurricanes, getting power back to 95 of every 100 customers who lost it took from about 3 days to nearly 3 weeks.[^ornl_repowrd_2022]

**What helps.** Have a flashlight for each person.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Keep a battery or hand-crank radio, and a backup battery for each phone.[^ready_gov_kit] Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours, and a full freezer for about two days.[^ready_gov_power_outages] If someone relies on a powered medical device or cold medicine, make a plan with their medical provider now.[^ready_gov_power_outages] You can also ask your power company to put you on its list for priority restoration.[^ready_gov_disability] A battery power station burns no fuel, so it makes no carbon monoxide.[^cdc_co_basics]

**What to avoid.** Never run a generator inside your home or garage, even with the doors and windows open. Keep it outside, more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents.[^cdc_co_basics] Put a carbon monoxide alarm on every level of your home.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven. Unplug electronics, because the power can come back with a surge that damages them.[^ready_gov_power_outages]
