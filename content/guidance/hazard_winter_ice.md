{frequency} Winter storms raise the risk of car crashes, hypothermia, frostbite, carbon monoxide poisoning, and heart attacks from overexertion.[^ready_gov_winter] Ice can bring down power lines, and a hard freeze can break water systems. In Texas, boil-water notices after winter storms lasted about a week in the typical case.[^shaffer_2026_texas_boil_notices]

**What helps.** Keep extra blankets, sleeping bags and warm coats, and pick one room to keep warm.[^cdc_winter_safety] If you cannot keep your home warm, go to a warming center. Dial 2-1-1 to find one.[^ready_gov_stay_safe_warm] Learn how to keep pipes from freezing, and test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms before winter.[^ready_gov_winter] Keep a car kit with jumper cables, sand, a flashlight, warm clothes, blankets, water and snacks, and keep the gas tank full.[^ready_gov_winter] Check on older neighbours often.[^cdc_winter_safety] Know the signs of hypothermia, such as shivering, confusion and slurred speech. A body temperature below 95°F is an emergency. Warm the center of the body first.[^ready_gov_winter] {if:avalanche}In avalanche country, get training, sign up for alerts from a Forest Service avalanche center, and carry a beacon, a probe and a small shovel.[^ready_gov_avalanche]{/if}

**What to avoid.** Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven. Use generators and grills only outdoors, away from windows.[^ready_gov_winter] Choose electric space heaters with automatic shut-off, and keep them 3 feet from anything that can burn.[^cdc_winter_safety][^usfa_heating_fires] Do not overdo it when you shovel snow.[^ready_gov_winter]
