{frequency} Dust storms are most common in the Southwest. They often arrive suddenly, as a wall of dust that can cut visibility on the road to almost nothing, though most last only a few minutes.[^nws_dust_storms] In the Southwest and Pacific Northwest, the soil can hold the fungus that causes Valley fever, a lung infection people get by breathing it in.[^cdc_valley_fever]

**What helps.** If dense dust is blowing across the road ahead, avoid driving into it. If it reaches you, pull off the pavement as far as you can, stop, turn off all your lights, set the parking brake and take your foot off the brake pedal.[^nws_dust_storms] If you cannot pull off, slow down, turn on your lights, sound the horn now and then, and follow the painted center line.[^nws_dust_storms]

**What to avoid.** Never stop in a lane of traffic. Do not leave your lights on once you are parked off the road: drivers behind may follow them and hit you.[^nws_dust_storms]
