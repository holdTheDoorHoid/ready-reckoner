{frequency} Severe weather can happen anytime, in any part of the country. Thunderstorms bring damaging winds, tornadoes, large hail, flash floods and lightning.[^ready_gov_severe_weather] Tornado winds can top 200 miles an hour.[^ready_gov_tornadoes]

**What helps.** Pick your shelter spot now. A safe room or storm shelter is best. The next best is a small, windowless room or basement on the lowest floor of a sturdy building.[^ready_gov_tornadoes] Sign up for your community's warnings, and learn what your sirens sound like.[^ready_gov_tornadoes] {if:tornado}Know the signs of a tornado: a rotating funnel cloud, a cloud of debris, or a roar like a freight train. In your shelter, protect your head and neck with your arms, and pile blankets or cushions around you.[^ready_gov_tornadoes]{/if} When thunder roars, go indoors. Get into a building or a car with a roof.[^ready_gov_thunderstorms_lightning]

**What to avoid.** Stay away from windows, doors and outside walls.[^ready_gov_tornadoes] {if:tornado}There is no safe place in a car during a tornado, only less dangerous ones. If you are caught, stay buckled in and keep your head below the windows.[^ready_gov_tornadoes]{/if} During a thunderstorm, avoid running water and electronics plugged into the wall.[^ready_gov_thunderstorms_lightning]
