{frequency} An earthquake, a storm or a transit shutdown can leave you far from home with roads or trains closed. You may need to stay where you are, or walk home.

**What helps.** Ready.gov has advised being ready to stay at work for at least 24 hours, with food, water, medicines and comfortable walking shoes.[^ready_gov_kit_2020] Keep a small get-home bag at work or in the car: water, snacks, a flashlight, a phone battery, a whistle, a dust mask and a paper map.[^ready_gov_kit] Plan your route home and walk part of it once. Road engineers plan for people walking about 2 to 2.4 miles an hour, so 10 miles can take 4 to 5 hours.[^fhwa_mutcd_walking_speed] In heat, drink about 1 cup of water every 15 to 20 minutes while you walk.[^cdc_niosh_heat_hydration] Include your children's school in your family plan, and agree who will pick them up.[^ready_gov_plan]

**What to avoid.** Do not force down water when you are not thirsty. Too much water can be harmful.[^cdc_yellow_book_heat_cold] In heat, do not drink more than 6 cups an hour.[^cdc_niosh_heat_hydration] If you are stuck in a car in a snowstorm, staying in the car is often safest. Run the engine about 10 minutes an hour with a window cracked, and keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow.[^cdc_winter_safety]
