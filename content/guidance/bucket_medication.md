{frequency} In a disaster, pharmacies and roads can close, and pharmacy computer systems can go down.[^rr_research_risk_model] Anyone who takes a daily medicine needs a way to keep taking it.

**What helps.** Refill early, while you still have a week left. The Red Cross suggests keeping a 7-day supply, and Florida advises at least two weeks.[^redcross_survival_kit][^florida_dem_medication] People with diabetes should pack supplies for at least 1 to 2 weeks.[^cdc_diabetes_emergencies] Talk to your doctor or pharmacist about building an emergency supply, and keep a written list of each person's medicines, doses and allergies.[^ready_gov_disability] Many states let pharmacists give an emergency refill, but the rules differ from state to state.[^healthcare_ready_refill_laws] After a declared disaster, a federal program can help people without insurance replace prescriptions.[^hhs_aspr_epap] If a medicine must stay cold, ask how long it can be kept warm, and plan a cooler.[^ready_gov_power_outages]

**What to avoid.** If the power is out for more than a day, throw away medicine that should be refrigerated, unless the label says otherwise.[^ready_gov_power_outages] Keep insulin cool but never frozen.[^cdc_insulin_emergency] Do not use expired medicines. Weak antibiotics can fail to treat an infection.[^fda_expired_medicines] Do not share antibiotics or save them for later.[^cdc_antibiotic_use]
