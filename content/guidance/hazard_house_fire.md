{frequency} In 2023 there were about 345,000 home fires in the United States, with 2,890 deaths and 10,400 injuries.[^usfa_residential_fires] In a fire, every second counts. A plan you have practiced gets you out fast.[^ready_gov_home_fires]

**What helps.** Put smoke alarms inside and outside every bedroom and on every level, and test them each month.[^usfa_smoke_alarms] Practice your escape plan twice a year, including finding your way out in the dark.[^ready_gov_home_fires] Sleep with bedroom doors closed. Teach children not to hide from firefighters.[^ready_gov_home_fires] If there is smoke, crawl low under it to your exit.[^ready_gov_home_fires] If you cannot get out, close the door, cover vents and cracks with cloth or tape, call 911, and signal from the window with a light-colored cloth or a flashlight.[^ready_gov_home_fires] Insurance and copies of your documents help you recover afterward.[^fema_effak]

**What to avoid.** Before you open a door, feel the door and the knob. If either is hot, or smoke is coming around it, keep it closed and use your second way out.[^ready_gov_home_fires] If a person or pet is still inside, get out and tell firefighters right away.[^ready_gov_home_fires] If your clothes catch fire, stop, drop and roll.[^ready_gov_home_fires]
