{frequency} Sometimes no water comes out of the tap, or officials say not to use it at all. Earthquakes, hard freezes, hurricanes and chemical spills can do this. After Hurricane Helene in 2024, Asheville, North Carolina, waited about seven weeks for safe tap water.[^epa_asheville_boil_notice_2024] If your well has an electric pump, every power cut is also a water cut.[^rr_research_risk_model]

**What helps.** Store at least one gallon per person per day, for drinking and keeping clean.[^ready_gov_water] Fill clean bottles or jugs with tap water and replace it every six months.[^cdc_water_storage] You can pick a level in the plan. About 3 liters a day covers drinking to survive, one gallon is the basic default, and about 4 gallons (15 liters) is the humanitarian minimum for drinking, cooking and washing.[^sphere_2018] A tank water heater holds 20 to 80 gallons of clean water.[^doe_water_heaters][^cdc_find_clean_water] For long outages, a filter and a source of raw water go further than more jugs.[^rr_research_risk_model] Filters do not remove viruses, so add a disinfectant such as chlorine dioxide after filtering.[^cdc_water_disinfection] A two-bucket toilet keeps waste safe when toilets cannot flush.[^rdpo_emergency_toilet]

**What to avoid.** Do not store water in containers that held chemicals.[^cdc_water_storage] Milk jugs seal poorly and turn brittle.[^church_emergency_prep_manual] Never drink pool or spa water. Use it only for washing and cleaning.[^cdc_find_clean_water] After a flood, do not drink from a private well until it has been disinfected and tested. Your health department can tell you how.[^cdc_well_disinfection]
