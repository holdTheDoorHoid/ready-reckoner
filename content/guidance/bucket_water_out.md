{frequency} Sometimes no water comes out of the tap, or officials say not to use it at all. Earthquakes, hard freezes, big storms and chemical spills can do this. A home with a well and an electric pump loses water in every power outage.

**What helps.** Store at least one gallon per person per day, for drinking and keeping clean.[^ready_gov_water] Fill clean bottles or jugs with tap water and replace it every six months.[^cdc_water_storage] The plan lets you pick a level. About 3 liters a day is survival, one gallon is the basic default, and about 15 liters is the humanitarian standard for comfort.[^sphere_2018] Your tank water heater also holds 20 to 80 gallons of clean water.[^doe_water_heaters][^cdc_find_clean_water] For long outages, a filter and a raw water source go further than more jugs. Add bleach or chlorine dioxide after filtering to kill viruses.[^cdc_water_disinfection] A two-bucket toilet keeps waste safe when toilets cannot flush.[^rdpo_emergency_toilet]

**What to avoid.** Do not store water in milk jugs or containers that held chemicals.[^cdc_water_storage] Never drink pool or spa water. It is only for washing and cleaning.[^cdc_find_clean_water] Do not bury bagged toilet waste.[^rdpo_emergency_toilet]
