{frequency} About 1 in 100 households had a burglary or trespassing in 2023.[^bjs_criminal_victimization_2023] After disasters, panic and looting are much rarer than news stories suggest. Most people help each other.[^clarke_2002_panic][^tierney_2006_disaster_myths]

**What helps.** Burglars look for homes that are easy targets. Lock up, and light the places where someone could hide.[^ncpc_home_safety] Work with your neighbours to look out for each other.[^ncpc_home_safety] Protect key accounts with a password manager and two-step login.[^ready_gov_cybersecurity] Learn a simple way to handle a tense moment: notice what is happening, say hello, judge the risk, and get help if you need it.[^cisa_deescalation] If an attack happens in a public place, getting away is the top priority.[^ready_gov_public_spaces]

**What to avoid.** After a disaster, be careful with people who show up offering fast repairs or help for a fee. Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.[^ftc_disaster_scams] Do not hide a spare key under the doormat or a planter. Leave it with a trusted neighbour.[^ncpc_home_safety]
