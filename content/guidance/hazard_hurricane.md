{frequency} Storm surge has been the leading cause of hurricane deaths in the United States.[^ready_gov_hurricanes] About one in three hurricanes that reached the U.S. coast since 1851 were category 3 or stronger.[^noaa_hurdat2] Power can be out for days, and water for weeks. After Hurricane Helene, Asheville waited about seven weeks for safe tap water.[^epa_asheville_boil_notice_2024]

**What helps.** Know your evacuation zone, your routes and where you would stay. If you live in a mandatory evacuation zone and officials tell you to leave, go right away.[^ready_gov_hurricanes] {if:not_home:apartment_high_rise|mobile_home}If you are not told to leave, shelter from the wind in a small inside room, closet or hallway away from windows, on the lowest floor that will not flood.[^ready_gov_hurricanes][^nws_hurricane_safety_brochure]{/if} {if:home:apartment_high_rise}Winds are stronger higher up. If you are not told to leave, take shelter on or below the 10th floor, in a small inside room or hallway away from windows and above any flooding. Elevators can stop when the power goes out.[^fema_hurricane_safety_tips_2012][^ready_gov_hurricanes_2014][^nws_hurricane_safety_brochure]{/if} {if:home:mobile_home}A mobile home is not safe in hurricane winds, however well it is tied down, so plan to leave.[^ready_gov_hurricanes_2014][^nws_hurricane_safety_brochure]{/if} Clear drains and gutters, bring in outdoor furniture, and think about storm shutters. Keep phones charged and have backup chargers.[^ready_gov_hurricanes] A flood policy typically takes up to 30 days to start, so buy it well before a storm.[^ready_gov_floods] If you would leave in an electric car, keep it fully charged all hurricane season.[^hcfl_ev_safety]

**What to avoid.** Never walk or drive through floodwater. One foot of moving water can sweep a car away.[^ready_gov_floods] Do not drive or charge an electric car that has been in floodwater. Salt water can start a battery fire.[^hcfl_ev_safety] Never run a generator inside your home or garage.[^cdc_co_basics]
