The easiest supplies to keep fresh are the ones you use. Store the foods your household already eats, use the oldest first, and replace them as you shop.[^fsis_shelf_stable][^church_home_storage_2007]

**What to check, and when.** Replace water you bottled yourself every six months.[^cdc_water_storage] Look through your pantry every few weeks and use cans you have had for a while.[^fsis_shelf_stable] Dates on most foods are about quality, not safety. Infant formula is an exception, and its date must be followed.[^fsis_shelf_stable] Test smoke alarms every month, and replace 9-volt batteries at least once a year.[^usfa_smoke_alarms] Check first-aid supplies for used or expired items.[^redcross_first_aid_kit] Update your documents at tax time, when the clocks change, on your birthday or at the new year.[^fema_effak] Rotate pet water and food in the go-kit every two months.[^aspca_disaster_prep]

**Make it a habit.** Tie checks to things you already do, like grocery trips or the clock change. New habits take time. In one study, they took from 18 to 254 days to become automatic, and missing a day now and then did not set people back.[^lally_2010_habits] One missed month is fine.

**What to avoid.** Do not store food in a hot garage or attic. Heat cuts shelf life sharply.[^usu_food_storage_booklet]
