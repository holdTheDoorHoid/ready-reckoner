Most people prepare for the disaster they can picture. In FEMA's 2024 survey, 47 of every 100 people said an attack in a public place would have a big impact where they live, but only 5 had been through one. Power outages were the most common experience: 22 of every 100 had been through one.[^fema_nhs_2024]

**How the plan works.** Ready Reckoner plans for consequences: no power, no safe tap water, can't get to a store, dangerous heat or cold, having to leave, and a few more. Emergency managers work the same way. They set targets for what a community must be able to do, whatever the cause.[^fema_cpg201_thira] Many hazards lead to the same few consequences. An ice storm, a heat wave and a regional blackout all mean no power. So one set of supplies covers many causes, and the plan never asks you to buy the same water twice.[^rr_research_risk_model]

**Where hazards still matter.** Hazards explain why a consequence is likely where you live, and how long it might last. A few hazards also add specific extras, such as Drop, Cover and Hold On practice for earthquakes or N95 masks for wildfire smoke.[^ready_gov_earthquakes][^ready_gov_wildfires]
