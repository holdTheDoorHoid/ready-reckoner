{frequency} News coverage can make unrest look bigger than it is. After Hurricane Katrina, media reports greatly exaggerated looting and lawlessness.[^tierney_2006_disaster_myths] In real emergencies, people in crowds often pull together and help each other.[^drury_2009_shared_identity] For most households, the likely effect is a curfew or closed stores for a few days (an estimate).[^rr_research_risk_model]

**What helps.** Your regular supplies cover a few days at home: food, water, medicine, cash and a charged phone.[^ready_gov_kit] Work with your neighbours to look out for each other.[^ncpc_home_safety] Learn a simple way to handle a tense moment: notice what is happening, say hello, judge the risk, and get help if you need it.[^cisa_deescalation] Wherever you go, notice the exits and places to hide.[^ready_gov_public_spaces] If an attack happens, get away if you can. If you can't, hide out of view behind something solid, lock the doors, turn off the lights and stay silent. Fight only as a last resort. Call 911 when you are safe.[^ready_gov_public_spaces]

**What to avoid.** Do not stop for your belongings. Leave them behind and get away. When police arrive, keep your hands empty and where they can see them.[^ready_gov_public_spaces]
