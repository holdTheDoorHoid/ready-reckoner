Hurricanes, winter storms and heat waves usually come with warning. Keep your phone charged when one is forecast, and use the time the same way each time.[^ready_gov_hurricanes]

**Before any storm**

- Charge phones, power banks and other batteries.[^texasready_hurricanes] {if:has:power_generator}Fuel the generator and check that it works.[^texasready_hurricanes]{/if}
- Fill the car's tank and get some cash, since card readers and cash machines need power.[^texasready_hurricanes] {if:has:power_station}Charge the power station.[^texasready_hurricanes]{/if} If you would leave in an electric car, charge it fully.[^hcfl_ev_safety]
- Refill prescriptions if you can.[^texasready_hurricanes]
- Fill the bathtub and other large containers with water for flushing and cleaning.[^texasready_hurricanes]
- Turn the fridge to its coldest setting and keep the doors shut.[^texasready_hurricanes] A full freezer keeps food cold for about 48 hours, a fridge for about four.[^ready_gov_power_outages]
- Bring in or tie down outdoor furniture and grills.[^ready_gov_hurricanes]
- Check your evacuation zone and your plan to leave.[^ready_gov_hurricanes]
- Check with neighbors, older adults and anyone who may need help.[^ready_gov_hurricanes]

**Before a hard freeze.** Let hot and cold water drip from a faucet at night, open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, and keep the heat at 55°F or higher. Make sure everyone knows how to shut off the water in case a pipe bursts.[^nws_aly_cold_safety]

**Before a heat wave.** Pick a place to cool down, such as a library, or ask your local health department about cooling options. Plan to check on family, older adults and neighbors. Fans do not lower body temperature.[^ready_gov_heat]
