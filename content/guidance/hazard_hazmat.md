{frequency} A chemical spill from a plant, a rail car or a truck can mean staying inside for a few hours, or leaving the area. It can also make tap water unsafe to drink, and boiling will not fix that.[^cdc_water_advisories]

**What helps.** Pick an above-ground room with as few windows and doors as possible.[^ready_gov_chemical] Keep plastic sheeting, duct tape and scissors to seal doors, windows and vents. You can measure and cut the sheeting ahead of time.[^ready_gov_shelter] If you are told to stay inside, bring pets in, close and lock doors and windows, close vents, and turn off air conditioning and fans. Then seal the room with plastic and tape.[^ready_gov_chemical] Listen to local radio or TV for instructions.[^ready_gov_chemical] Keep stored water, since you may be told not to drink from the tap.[^cdc_water_advisories]

**What to avoid.** If you are outside, do not walk toward the spill. Move away upwind, and find the closest building to shelter in.[^ready_gov_chemical] Do not leave your shelter until officials say it is safe.[^ready_gov_chemical] If a chemical gets on you and help is not close, take off your clothes, bag them, and wash with soap and water.[^ready_gov_chemical]
