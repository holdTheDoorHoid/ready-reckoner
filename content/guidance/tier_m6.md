Six months enters a plan only for rare, severe events, and usually only when you choose a cautious setting. One example is the Oregon coast after a Cascadia earthquake. Oregon's resilience plan expects power to take 3 to 6 months to come back there.[^oregon_resilience_plan_2013]

**What is in it.** At this length, the plan shifts from stockpiles to ways of making do. That means a filter and a raw water source instead of hundreds of gallons, and staple foods that store well.[^rr_research_risk_model] Store dry staples somewhere cool. Wheat kept its quality for 25 years in a cool basement, but only 5 years in a hot garage or attic.[^usu_food_storage_booklet] Six months of expenses is the top of the usual emergency-fund range.[^finra_financial_foundations] Neighbours matter too. After Japan's 2011 tsunami, towns with stronger community ties had lower death rates.[^aldrich_sawada_2015]

**When it is enough.** If your plan shows this tier, it is because one named event drives your answer. The plan says which event it is and shows your plan with and without it.[^rr_research_risk_model]
