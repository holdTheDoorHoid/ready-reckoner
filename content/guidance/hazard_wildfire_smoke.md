{frequency} Wildfire smoke can travel far from the fire itself. It irritates the eyes, nose, throat and lungs, and it is hardest on children, older adults and people with heart or lung disease.[^epa_wildfire_indoor_air][^epa_clean_room][^epa_protect_lungs_2026]

**What helps.** If anyone has heart or lung disease, ask their health care provider now what to do on smoky days. If someone has asthma, make sure they have an asthma action plan.[^epa_wildfire_indoor_air] Keep at least five days of food and medicine at home, so you can stay in.[^epa_clean_room] When smoke is forecast, check the air quality on AirNow or your local news, and set up your clean room.[^epa_wildfire_indoor_air] Staying indoors is the best protection. Keep two-strap respirators for anyone who has to go out.[^epa_protect_lungs_2026]

**What to avoid.** Avoid hard exercise while it is smoky.[^epa_wildfire_indoor_air] Do not stay in a home that is too hot or still smoky inside: go to a friend's home out of the smoke, or a public cleaner-air shelter.[^epa_clean_room]
