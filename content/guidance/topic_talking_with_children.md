Children cope better when they understand what is happening and what they can do to help keep themselves and their family safe.[^aap_talking_to_children] Everyone can help prepare, including young children and teens.[^ready_gov_kids_families]

**Before anything happens.** Let children help build the kit and practice the plan.[^ready_gov_kids_families] Make sure each child knows your family meeting place and has a contact card.[^ready_gov_plan] Practice the fire escape plan together twice a year.[^ready_gov_home_fires]

**When something happens.** Start by asking what they already know and what questions they have. Give simple, concrete facts without scary details. For a young child, that might be: "A tree branch fell on the power lines, so the lights are off." Tell them about the many people working to help, and the steps being taken to keep them safe.[^aap_talking_to_children]

**What to avoid.** Limit news coverage. If children watch, watch with them and answer their questions.[^aap_talking_to_children] Reassure them when you honestly can, but do not give false reassurance. Let them know it is all right to be upset. If you feel overwhelmed, get support from another adult first. Helping others in the community can ease a child's sense of helplessness.[^aap_talking_to_children]
