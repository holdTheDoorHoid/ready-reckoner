After an emergency, you may need to get by on your own for several days.[^ready_gov_kit] For years, Ready.gov asked households to have supplies for at least 72 hours.[^ready_gov_kit_2020] Denmark gives the reason plainly: if people can manage for three days, the authorities can focus on stabilising the situation and helping those most in need.[^dema_prepared_for_crises]

**What is in it.** At least one gallon of water per person per day for three days.[^cdc_water_storage] Food your household already eats, with a manual can opener.[^ready_gov_food] A flashlight for each person, a battery or hand-crank radio, spare batteries, a first-aid kit, and a backup battery for each phone.[^ready_gov_kit] A few days of medicines and a written list of them.[^ready_gov_disability] A go-bag for each person, in case you have to leave.[^ready_gov_evacuation] Next comes a get-home bag for each person who commutes.

**When it is enough.** Three days covers most power cuts and boil-water notices. In 2024, 88 of every 100 people who had set supplies aside said they would last more than three days.[^fema_nhs_2024] If any of your targets is longer than three days, the plan moves on to two weeks.
