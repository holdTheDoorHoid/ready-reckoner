Plan how you will reach each other, and reconnect if you are split up, before phones and networks are busy or down.[^ready_gov_plan]

**Four ways to reach each other.** Emergency planners call this a PACE plan: a primary way, an alternate, a contingency and an emergency way. Each one takes over when the one before it fails.[^cisa_pace_flyer] For a household it can be:

1. Primary: call.
2. Alternate: text. A text may get through when a call does not, and it saves battery.[^ready_gov_earthquakes]
3. Contingency: everyone texts one out-of-state contact, who passes the news along.[^ready_gov_earthquakes]
4. Emergency: go to the place you agreed to meet if you get separated.[^ready_gov_earthquakes]

**Wallet cards.** Give each person a card with every household member's phone number, an out-of-town contact, a meeting place near home and one outside the neighborhood, any medical needs, and the numbers for your doctor, pharmacy and insurance.[^ready_gov_family_comm_card] Keep it in a wallet or phone case, and learn two or three numbers by heart in case the phone is lost or dead.[^ollam_2022_lawyer_passport_locksmith_gun]

**School and child care.** Every place your child spends time should have an emergency plan. Keep your contact details, the list of adults allowed to pick up your child, custody papers and medical needs up to date there. After an emergency, follow official directions about where to reunite.[^fema_back_to_school_2026]
